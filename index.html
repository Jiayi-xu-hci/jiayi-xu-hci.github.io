I lead research on the body as an interface. With my students and collaborators, I build interactive systems that sense, stimulate and extend the human body, spanning multisensory feedback, physiological sensing and wearable robotics.

Our work appears in leading journals and conferences in HCI, virtual reality and haptics, and has reached the public through CES, the world’s largest tech show, and Japan’s public broadcaster NHK. I am principal investigator of two JSPS KAKENHI grants and received my Ph.D. in Engineering from the University of Tsukuba in 2023.

Research

Computers have learned to speak to our eyes and ears. To the rest of the body, they remain almost silent.

Warmth, touch, posture and inner state shape how we experience the world, yet interactive systems rarely treat them as media to design with. I aim to make the body itself a first-class interface, one that can render sensations, read a person’s state and extend what the body can do, starting from quantitative models of human perception.

  1. 01 · Render

    Multisensory feedback

    Can touch and temperature become as immediate and expressive as light and sound? I design thermal and tactile displays grounded in models of skin and perception.

  2. 02 · Read

    Human-state sensing

    What can the body tell an interface? I sense subtle physiological and motor signals so that systems understand a person’s state without explicit input.

  3. 03 · Extend

    Embodied augmentation

    How do we build technology the body accepts as its own? I study how people move, extend and adapt with robotic limbs and haptic guidance.

Looking ahead. I want to close the loop between these threads: body-adaptive interfaces that read a person’s state, render sensations tuned to their perception and reshape what their body can do in real time, validated in communication, media and healthcare with industry partners.
