Jiayi Xu 許 佳禕

I design interactions that understand the human body — and adapt to it.

Project Assistant Professor
Information Somatics Lab (Inami Lab), RCAST, The University of Tokyo

My research starts from quantitatively understanding human perception and bodily states, and turns that understanding into new interaction principles — validated from perception experiments and models, through devices and user studies, to public deployment. Temperature is my core medium: I build non-contact, high-speed thermal interfaces, and extend the same perception-centred approach to embodied interaction and sensing of human states.

C measures the body → A presents to the senses → B reshapes what the body can do → back to C

Method · how a thermal interface is built

  1. 01Stimulus generationVortex airflow, LED radiation, mist evaporation
  2. 02Physical predictionSkin-temperature model, RMSE 0.16 °C
  3. 03Perceptual controlTemporal patterns: cold without net cooling
  4. 04Wearable integrationHMD, headphones: −2.6 °C / 5 s, 0.66 s onset
  5. 05DeploymentIndustry research, patents, CES 2026

PILLAR A · JOINT RESEARCH WITH SUNTORY

MoHeat Evo

A directional thermal headphone for spatial audio. It brings the whole method together: LED heating and ethanol-mist cooling (01), controlled per ear (02–03), integrated into a headphone (04), and shown to the public at CES 2026 (05).
